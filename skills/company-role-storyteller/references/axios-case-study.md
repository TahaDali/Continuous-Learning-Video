## Scene 1: why the business needs a shared system

Emma wants to reach business decision makers. Michael needs to understand her goals, budget, and timing before proposing a package. Olivia later needs the agreed dates, contacts, and deliverables. Finance needs accurate commercial records. Leadership needs to know how much business is possible, contracted, delivered, billed, and collected.

The story introduces the CRM because these people need coordinated information. Explain an account as a company record, a contact as a person, and an opportunity as a possible deal when each becomes relevant. Official vendor documentation must establish actual platform capabilities; the fictional story cannot prove the employer's configuration.

The teaching gain: the learner understands why a field or workflow matters before hearing a list of administrative features.

## Scene 2: investigate a number before changing it

The fictional manager asks why a dashboard says $200,000 while the approved agreement says $150,000. The analyst first determines whether the report means pipeline, bookings, or another measure. They inspect the available history, agreement, related records, filters, and relevant integration or automation behavior.

One possible explanation is that the old proposal amount remained after the package was reduced. The analyst checks that explanation, establishes the intended source and definition with the appropriate owners, and determines whether other records are affected. They communicate evidence, limits, and next steps before recommending a lasting fix.

The teaching gain: diagnosis and shared definitions come before a convenient edit.

## Scene 3: solve a disagreement through better requirements

Olivia needs complete information before accepting a handoff. Michael needs to record a legitimately signed deal while some delivery details remain unfinished. Their needs are compatible once **commercial agreement** and **operational readiness** are treated as different states.

The analyst investigates the existing process, agrees on necessary information, and designs an appropriate readiness condition. A candidate automation is tested for incomplete information, repeated triggers, ownership, access, reopened records, and unintended effects. Users try it in an appropriate practice environment; any real release would follow the organization's actual authorization and change process.

The transcript's fictional completeness measure moved from **62% to 93%**, a **31 percentage-point increase**. This is invented demonstration data, not an Axios achievement or target. A credible analysis would define the denominator, compare equivalent periods, and examine other contributing changes.

The teaching gain: a good analyst improves a shared outcome instead of choosing one department's demand without understanding the other.

## Scene 4: evaluate evidence rather than impressive claims

Three fictional applicants illustrate distinct evidence:

- **Emily:** direct Salesforce administration and production support, with developing commercial analysis experience.
- **Daniel:** commercial operations, SQL, Python, and reporting experience, with limited direct Salesforce administration.
- **Sophia:** apparently stronger overlap across platform and revenue operations responsibilities, pending verification of her personal contributions.

A hypothetical migration exercise introduces 1,000 source opportunities, 20 possible duplicates, 40 missing dates, and a $250,000 aggregate discrepancy. These are fictional inputs. Strong reasoning checks acceptance criteria, stable identifiers, legitimate repeated customers, stage-dependent date requirements, definitions, relationships, permissions, and record-level differences before approving or “cleaning” the migration.

The teaching gain: transferable strengths count, direct gaps still matter, and the strongest-looking applicant can lose credibility if they cannot substantiate their work. Never treat this as Axios's actual candidate pool, hiring test, or scoring rubric.

## Carry the method into the next part or another industry

For Part V, follow the employee from the first manager conversation through baseline work, an early mistake, a bounded project, stakeholder feedback, a six-month review, and first-year evidence. Label the whole employee journey as a simulation; use the current posting to bound the responsibilities. Then explain possible advancement through demonstrated scope, with no promised title or timetable.

For a different company, replace the business economics, customer journey, systems, and decision constraints. Retain the sequence of discovery, coherent fictional case, evidence-based judgment, realistic work, and honest uncertainty. Do not carry over a media advertising pipeline where it does not fit.
