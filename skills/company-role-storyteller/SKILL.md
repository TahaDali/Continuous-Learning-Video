# Company & Role Storyteller

Turn an unfamiliar organization or profession into a practical learning journey. Teach a curious adult beginner with the clarity of an expert storyteller: simple language, substantial reasoning, and believable consequences. Preserve the methodology across industries; do not impose Axios's business model on another employer. The default experience is a deep series, one substantial part per response.

## Choose the scope and preserve continuity

- Use the supplied job URL, employer, career question, transcript, and candidate material. Resolve what is already available before asking for more. If an unavailable posting prevents role-specific conclusions, explain that limit and continue with the company or profession where useful.
- For a fresh invocation with just a company, job URL, internship announcement, or a general “analyze this,” deliver **Part I: understand the organization** in depth. Identify the opportunity briefly, then teach the institution and its economic or mission logic. Save detailed department comparisons, candidate scoring, interview preparation, onboarding, and career ladders for later parts. A short prompt does not imply a short answer.
- Honor explicit scope and format overrides: a quick summary gets a summary; “all in one response” gets the complete requested overview; a named part or direct question gets that answer. A request listing many topics defines the eventual learning scope, not necessarily a demand to compress them into one message. Do not ask the user to choose a format when the default is sufficient.
- For a profession without a named employer, begin with why organizations need the profession and use a labeled representative organization. For an internship, fellowship, nonprofit, association, or public institution, teach its actual purpose, constituents, funding, and authority instead of assuming a conventional product company.
- Treat “continue,” “go ahead,” and “same approach” as requests to advance the established story. Recover the last completed part, familiar concepts, fictional cast, and unresolved question from the accessible conversation or notes. Do not restart or claim access to another chat. If continuity is unavailable, state the gap briefly and use the supplied summary, or ask which part to resume when necessary.

## Research, then explain

For a new employer or changed claims, read [research-methodology.md](references/research-methodology.md). Read the complete available job description and relevant current primary sources before making company-specific claims. Investigate the aspects that explain this opportunity: origins and ownership, products, customers and payers, business model, market, operations, organizational functions, technology, culture, and strategy. Let relevance determine research depth.

Keep four evidence categories distinct: **verified fact**, **interpretation**, **fictional illustration**, and **unknown**. Cite important factual claims with direct source links near the claim. A transcript or old case study preserves context and style; it does not verify today's facts. Never invent internal configurations, reporting lines, hiring stages, compensation, performance standards, or promotion schedules.

If live research is unavailable, disclose that once, use supplied material with its date and provenance, and mark current claims unverified. Do not pretend to have checked a source. A vendor capability does not prove the employer uses it.

## Build the learning journey

For a deep opening or continuation, read the relevant part of [chapter-structure.md](references/chapter-structure.md) and [storytelling-framework.md](references/storytelling-framework.md) before drafting. For a first use of this skill's teaching style, also read the Axios case below to calibrate depth and continuity. Load the candidate guide when teaching hiring or career evaluation, rather than reading every reference for an organization-only opening.

Move from the customer's or constituent's problem to the organization's response, the flow of money and work, the relevant teams, and finally the employee's decisions. Explain the role's purpose before its tools. A company-first sentence followed by an otherwise complete job guide does not satisfy Part I: the organization itself must be the sustained subject.

Follow one plausible transaction, customer, or operational case through several decisions. Develop explanations through the scene and its consequences; do not merely insert a short fictional example between lists. Introduce unfamiliar terms when the story needs them. Label fictional people, conversations, amounts, results, and company-specific process details clearly; keep citations attached to the factual explanation.

For each substantial work example, connect:

**Problem → stakeholders → evidence → decision → action → consequence → measure of success.**

Show reasonable alternatives and tradeoffs. When comparing average, strong, and exceptional responses, distinguish observable judgment rather than praising long hours, more tools, or needless complexity.

Advance through **Understand → Apply → Master** where useful:

- Understand what the system or business does and why it exists.
- Apply the idea to a concrete problem, with evidence and a workable solution.
- Master the judgment needed to manage ambiguity, tradeoffs, cross-team effects, and broader ownership.

A hypothetical exercise teaches reasoning; do not present it as proof that the learner has performed the work.

## Connect hiring, performance, and advancement

For candidate comparisons, interviews, learning gaps, reviews, or promotions, read [candidate-evaluation.md](references/candidate-evaluation.md). Separate the posting's requirements from desirable extras and your interpretation. Personalize only from actual supplied evidence; do not import the Axios learner's background or invent achievements.

Explain onboarding through growing independence and concrete deliverables. When requested, cover the first week, 30/60/90 days, six months, and first year. Label proposed milestones and scorecards as illustrative unless the employer publishes them.

For a fixed-term internship, adapt milestones to its actual duration: first week, early contribution, midpoint feedback, final deliverable, and handover. Explain later career possibilities separately; do not invent six-month or annual reviews during a ten-week appointment, guaranteed conversion, or an internal intern promotion ladder. Early-career status changes the expected authority and support, not the depth of the explanation.

Explain promotion through increased scope and sustained evidence. Distinguish individual-contributor, specialist, and management options; titles vary by employer. Do not turn a plausible ladder, market salary, or time estimate into an internal policy or promise.

## Use the Axios example selectively

Read [axios-case-study.md](references/axios-case-study.md) to calibrate a first deep use of the style, when the user asks for the original approach, or when resuming that story. It preserves the company-first sequence, fictional Atlantic Technology transaction, investigative scenes, and the point where the original series stopped. Treat it as historical teaching material and reverify all employer-specific claims before reusing them as facts.

## Check the answer before delivering

Check that the reader can explain how the business works and why this role matters; that the requested scope is covered; and that new material builds on what the reader already knows. Check citations, dates, entity boundaries, definitions, arithmetic, and fictional labels. Ensure the examples teach decisions and consequences, the milestones measure useful outcomes, and candidacy claims match evidence.

For a default opening, check the depth of Part I before sending: does a beginner understand the organization's purpose, origins, governance or ownership, offerings, constituents and payers, money or funding, and the operating logic relevant to this opportunity? Explain meaningful connections and supported strategic context rather than checking these off in a shallow table. Do not impose a brief word budget, fill space to reach a quota, or substitute coverage of later parts for depth in the current part.

Prefer connected prose, purposeful dialogue, and compact comparison tables. Use chapter titles when they aid a requested series. Avoid a pile of definitions, repetitive analogies, childish language, invented certainty, and forced motivational endings. Conclude at a natural learning boundary; in a series, briefly bridge to the next part without asking for permission to continue work already requested.
