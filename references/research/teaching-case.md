# Fictional teaching case: Learning Press

This newly authored example is entirely fictional. Learning Press, its employees,
customers, dialogue, amounts and operating practices do not describe any employer.
It illustrates an explanatory method; it is not a fact sheet or a course template.

## Begin with the organization

Learning Press publishes a weekly science newsletter. Readers want dependable
explanations; sponsors pay to reach that audience. Editors, commercial staff and
operations therefore need different evidence of success. An editor asks whether a
reader can understand a finding. A sponsor asks what was actually delivered. The
operations analyst makes commitments and delivery records legible across teams.

Teach those relationships before introducing software fields. Verify the actual
organization's purpose, ownership and economics separately when adapting the method.

## Follow one commitment through the work

A fictional sponsor requests four newsletter placements. The sales team proposes a
package, the editor checks available inventory, and operations records a confirmed
schedule. A tentative proposal and a confirmed booking are different states. The
same commitment later produces delivery evidence and an invoice; neither alone
proves the invoice has been paid.

Build the explanation around an identifiable placement record. Keep its original
schedule visible while revealing approval, delivery and billing. This makes the
relationship understandable without forcing every business into this example.

## Investigate a disagreement

A report shows five placements while the signed fictional agreement specifies
four. The analyst asks what the report counts, inspects record identity and revision
history, and tests whether a superseded proposal remains in the total. The extra
placement is a hypothesis to explain, not a number to delete automatically.

Compare at least two plausible causes, show evidence that rejects one, and agree on
the authoritative business definition before changing a workflow. Verify both the
corrected case and an unaffected case. Record what the checks cannot establish.

## Teach judgment and growing responsibility

A fictional new analyst first works under review, later owns a bounded reconciliation,
and eventually helps colleagues handle exceptions. A migration exercise can compare
record identities and relationships rather than treating equal row counts as proof
of correctness. A hiring scene can ask candidates to explain their own contribution
and name what evidence would change their view.

Onboarding milestones and advancement remain illustrations. Use the real posting's
scope and appointment duration; do not invent internal hiring or promotion policy.

## Adapt the mechanism

Keep the sequence of organizational purpose, operational tension, competing
explanations, evidence, decision and consequence. Replace the people, economics,
artifacts and constraints for the subject. A manufacturing tolerance, a public-service
eligibility decision and a financial forecast need different visible mechanisms.
