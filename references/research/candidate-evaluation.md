# Candidate evaluation and career reasoning

Use for an ideal-candidate profile, personal fit analysis, interview simulation, portfolio plan, performance review, or promotion discussion.

## Translate the job description into evidence

Separate four things:

1. **Published requirements:** preserve exact experience, credential, timing, and location qualifications.
2. **Desirable extras:** retain the employer's distinction between preferred and mandatory.
3. **Inferred success factors:** explain why a responsibility suggests a useful capability; identify the inference.
4. **Unknown internal criteria:** hiring rubric, actual interview sequence, team support, and tolerance for trainable gaps remain unknown unless established.

Build a compact matrix when it makes the comparison clearer:

| Responsibility | Evidence that would support readiness | Boundary or question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Own a business system | Explain an actual configuration, user problem, access decision, testing, and operating result | Who owned production release, and what did the candidate personally do? |
| Produce decision-useful analysis | Reconcile definitions and sources, identify a finding, and explain the resulting decision | Was the finding acted on? How were accuracy and attribution checked? |
| Improve a workflow | Show the prior problem, stakeholders, alternatives, implementation, and outcome | Did it merely shift work to another team? |
| Support a migration or implementation | Show mapping, identifiers, reconciliation, exceptions, relationships, acceptance criteria, and assigned responsibility | A clean row count alone does not establish a successful migration |
| Train and support colleagues | Demonstrate useful explanation, adoption, documentation, and feedback | Is there evidence beyond attendance at a training session? |

Adapt the evidence to the role. An AI enablement employee may need adoption design and evaluation; a financial analyst may need reconciliation and control discipline; a platform administrator needs direct configuration and operating judgment. Do not substitute generic “data skills” for a central domain requirement.

Distinguish experience that can overlap in time from sequential years. Distinguish certification at entry from certification allowed within a specified period. Credential names and exam codes can change; verify them before giving current credential advice.

## Describe an ideal person without creating a fantasy candidate

Connect each quality to a job decision. “Communicates clearly” becomes “can explain a forecast discrepancy to a sales leader and agree on the definition with Finance.” “Ownership” becomes “resolves the assigned problem, reports its limits, and escalates decisions outside their authority.”

An ideal candidate at an early-career level still needs supervision, context, and domain learning. Do not silently raise the role to architect, executive, AI engineer, accountant, and salesperson combined. Separate essential day-one independence from skills that can reasonably develop after hiring.

Compare fictional candidates with plausible strengths and gaps, not fixed personality rankings. A tool specialist may need commercial context; an operations analyst may need platform practice; a hybrid applicant may still fail to substantiate claimed work. State what new evidence would change the assessment. Do not assign unsupported hiring probabilities.

## Personalize only from actual evidence

Use the user's current resume, stated projects, portfolio, credentials, and conversation. Distinguish “you report” from independently examined evidence where material. If background is unavailable, provide a conditional comparison rather than inventing a biography or delaying an otherwise useful role explanation.

Classify each relevant area as demonstrated direct experience, transferable experience, learning or practice evidence, or an unconfirmed gap. A practice environment can demonstrate a skill but does not establish production ownership. SQL or Python experience may support learning a CRM; it does not prove knowledge of that CRM's permissions or release process.

Recommend the smallest credible way to close an important evidence gap: a worked diagnosis, sandbox configuration, reconciled dataset, user-training demonstration, or carefully scoped portfolio case. Keep project work relevant to the job's actual problem. Do not recommend an elaborate AI product merely because the learner likes AI.

When rewriting example achievements, use verified facts or explicit fill-in prompts. Do not invent numerical improvements, certifications, clients, employment dates, or team accomplishments attributed to one person.

## Simulate an interview and a practical exercise

Label the interview sequence and questions as hypothetical unless the employer discloses them. A plausible simulation can include role understanding, business judgment, practical skills, and evidence verification; it does not establish how this company interviews.

Useful questions expose a decision:

- “Two reports disagree before a leadership meeting. How do you investigate and communicate the uncertainty?”
- “A requested automation helps one department but blocks another. How do you define the requirement?”
- “What did you personally build, what went wrong, and how did you verify the result?”
- “Which part of this system would you need help with in your first month?”

Explain what makes an answer credible: clarify the measure, inspect relevant evidence, consider alternatives, act within authority, test proportionately, and verify the outcome. A candidate should be able to defend why each check matters, not merely recite a checklist.

For a practical exercise provide the goal, available data or environment, relevant constraints, expected deliverable, and observable assessment criteria. Keep it bounded. For example, ask the candidate to diagnose a reconciliation discrepancy and propose acceptance criteria, using fictional records. Assess whether they identify uncertainties and protect legitimate distinctions before changing data.

Use authorized practice environments or supplied sample data for demonstrations. A teaching request does not authorize changes to a real employer's system, contacting a recruiter, submitting an application, or claiming someone else's work.

## Connect performance and promotion to outcomes

For an outcome metric, identify the baseline, denominator, observation window, source, owner, and relevant confounders. Compare like with like. A shift from 62% to 93% is 31 percentage points, not 31 percent. Avoid causal claims if seasonality, workload, staffing, or another change could explain the result.

Discuss a balanced evaluation: accuracy and reliability; useful delivery; adoption; stakeholder collaboration; judgment; documentation; and the scope actually owned. Do not reward speed while ignoring recurring defects, access mistakes, or work shifted to others.

For promotion, ask what becomes bigger or harder: ambiguity, consequences, users served, systems owned, cross-team coordination, mentoring, people management, budgets, or strategy. Use a portfolio of sustained evidence and candid gaps. A possible next title should reflect that scope and be labeled as illustrative unless documented for the employer.

When asked about pay, separate the posted range, verified employer bands, and external market comparisons. Check location, currency, date, base versus total compensation, and seniority. If evidence is missing, explain what is unknown rather than generating an automatic salary increase at each rung.
