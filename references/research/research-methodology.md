# Research methodology

Use for a new organization or role, for a changed job posting, or when a previous chapter's factual basis needs refreshing.

## Establish the exact subject

Resolve the legal or operating entity, product, location, job title, and job identifier where available. Separate a parent, subsidiary, spin-off, similarly named business, and product brand. A parent's size, a sister company's revenue, or another location's salary may not describe this employer.

Read the full posting, including requirements, reporting line, work arrangement, compensation, certification timing, and application status. Preserve the difference between a requirement and a preference, and between owning a task and supporting another owner. Do not add overlapping experience requirements together unless the wording requires separate periods.

If the link is closed or inaccessible:

- Say what could actually be retrieved. A missing page does not by itself prove the job was filled.
- Use a supplied copy as a dated description, not proof of a current opening.
- Keep researching the business and role family. Request the posting text only if missing responsibilities materially prevent the requested analysis.
- Treat a comparable vacancy as a comparator and identify the difference; never silently replace the requested job.

## Build an evidence map

Use browsing or connected research tools available in the session. Prefer the source that actually establishes the claim, rather than collecting links for appearance.

| Question | Useful sources | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Why does the company exist? | Company history, founding announcements, product explanations | Founders' stated aims are their account, not proof of results |
| What does it sell, and who pays? | Product, pricing, advertising, customer, and service pages; filings | A user, customer, beneficiary, and payer can be different people |
| Who owns and leads it? | Dated company and owner announcements, leadership pages, filings | Acquisition valuation, revenue, profit, and annualized run rate differ |
| How does work move? | The target posting, related jobs, public process documentation | Infer a plausible operating model; do not invent the internal org chart |
| Which technology matters? | Employer disclosures and official vendor documentation | Product capabilities are not evidence of deployment or enabled modules |
| What is the market context? | Filings, public competitors' materials, credible dated reporting | Distinguish business competition from competition for candidates |
| What does the employer value? | Published values, job expectations, public strategy statements | Stated culture is not independently verified daily experience |
| How might the role develop? | Published career frameworks, adjacent public roles, sourced market data | External titles and pay do not establish an internal ladder |

Use reputable reporting for context not established by primary sources, particularly private-company developments, and identify estimates. Employee reviews can suggest questions to investigate; they do not establish universal working conditions. For technical explanations rely on official documentation and primary technical sources.

Research enough to explain the requested decisions. Do not turn a focused role explanation into an exhaustive corporate history. For regulated contexts, verify relevant current rules with the responsible authority when explaining them; avoid giving unsourced legal or financial guidance.

## Track evidence without burdening the reader

Maintain working notes with the claim, evidence category, source URL or supplied document, publication or effective date, and material limit. Show a compact evidence table only when it helps the user assess uncertainty. No database or separate file is required unless the user wants durable research notes.

| Category | Example wording |
| --- | --- |
| Verified fact | “The posting lists responsibility for CRM migration.” Link the posting. |
| Interpretation | “That combination suggests the role will balance user support with project work.” Explain the inference. |
| Fictional illustration | “In our fictional campaign, the original proposal is $200,000.” |
| Unknown | “The posting does not disclose the current migration phase.” |

A citation supports only what the page actually says. Do not cite the job advertisement as proof of your fictional manager's words or a suggested 90-day plan. When fiction uses real capabilities, separate the sourced capability from the invented implementation.

Keep dates explicit for unstable facts such as openings, executives, ownership, headcount, products, salary ranges, and certifications. A dated acquisition announcement establishes an event on that date; verify later changes before describing present ownership. If sources disagree, compare their dates, entities, definitions, and authority; report unresolved disagreement without filling it with a guess.

## Follow value, work, and money

Prepare a causal chain before explaining tools:

**Customer or beneficiary problem → offering → buyer or funder → payment or funding → delivery → evidence of value → repeat relationship.**

Then locate the employee: what information, decision, process, or risk must this person improve so the chain works?

Distinguish monetary measures when they matter:

- **Pipeline:** possible business, subject to stage definitions and uncertainty.
- **Bookings:** contracted business under an agreed operational definition.
- **Invoicing:** a bill has been issued.
- **Cash:** payment has been collected.
- **Recognized revenue:** accounting revenue under applicable policy and standards.
- **Profit:** revenue less relevant expenses under a specified measure.

Do not assume those values coincide. A weighted pipeline calculation is an illustration based on chosen probabilities, not a guarantee or a complete forecasting model. Define currency, period, gross versus net basis, and cohort where material.

For a bank, follow an appropriately chosen service and its operating obligations, rather than treating all banking as advertising or software subscriptions. For a public or nonprofit employer, follow beneficiaries, funding, service delivery, and mission outcomes. For an internal enablement role, distinguish external customer value from how employees improve delivery.

## Finish research honestly

Before writing, resolve the claims needed for the requested chapter and list important unknowns. Answer from the available evidence when missing private details do not prevent useful teaching. Explain how those unknowns could be clarified through public sources or interview questions; do not pretend to know the answer.
